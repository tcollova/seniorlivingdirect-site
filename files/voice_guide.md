# Senior Living Direct — Voice Guide

How we sound, what we say, what we don't.

---

## The voice in one sentence

**Plainspoken, specific, occasionally dry, and grounded in 25 years of doing the work — not selling the work.**

---

## Five rules

### 1. Concrete beats abstract
We say what we mean and we use specific examples. We don't hide behind category words.

- ❌ "We leverage cutting-edge solutions to drive transformation."
- ✅ "We track competitor move-in specials and tell you within hours, not quarters."

### 2. Confident, not cocky
We're proud of being good at this. We don't need to brag.

- ❌ "We're the leading senior living agency in North America."
- ✅ "We do one thing, and we've been doing it a long time."

### 3. Warmth without sentimentality
This is senior living — emotion lives in the work. We can be human without being saccharine.

- ❌ "Our heart pours into every cherished resident's golden years."
- ✅ "Our love for seniors fuels our excitement for building campaigns around the distinct people, places, and moments that make a community delightful and marketable."

### 4. Dry humor is welcome
Self-aware, slightly understated. Never punching down.

- ✅ "Need something not listed here? Ask. After twenty-five years, there's a good chance we've done it."
- ✅ "We buy media like we're spending our own money, because we're spending yours."

### 5. AI talk requires special care
Whenever AI is the topic, lead with humans, expertise, and craft. Tech is supporting context.

- ❌ "Our AI-powered platform revolutionizes senior living marketing."
- ✅ "AI is a part of how we work now, and we're good at it. But it's a multiplier, not a shortcut."

## Vocabulary

### Words we use
- *Community* (preferred over "facility")
- *Resident* (not "client" or "customer")
- *Adult children* (the second audience — important to name)
- *Prospect* (for marketing-funnel context)
- *Campaign*, *piece*, *tour*, *move-in*, *census*
- *Strategic*, *creative*, *grounded*, *thoughtful*, *practical*

### Phrases that sound like us
- "We do one thing"
- "Quality over quantity"
- "Your voice, not ours"
- "Strategy before tactics"
- "We've been at this a long time"
- "After twenty-five years..."
- "The right fit, not just a signed contract"
- "Real utility, not generic automation"

### Words to avoid
- *Leverage*, *synergy*, *robust*, *cutting-edge*
- *Transform*, *revolutionize*, *disrupt*, *unlock*
- *AI-powered*, *next-generation*, *best-in-class*
- *Solutions* (when "things we do" works fine)
- *Stakeholders* (say who we mean — staff, residents, families)
- *Customer-centric* (just *care about people*)

## Sentence rhythm

### Vary length
Short sentences land harder when surrounded by longer ones. Mix them.

### Em dashes — okay but rare
The current website uses them sparingly and so should we. Don't lean on them as a default rhythm device. (Note: Claude tends to over-use em dashes; in copy for SLD, prefer periods, semicolons, or new sentences.)

### Don't be afraid of a fragment
For emphasis. Sparingly.

### Read it aloud
If a sentence is hard to read out loud, rewrite it. We talk like real people because real people read our work.

## Voice across formats

### Headlines
Short, declarative, often a contrast or a quiet claim.
- *"Strategic. Creative. Human-led."*
- *"AI informed by senior living experts. Not the other way around."*
- *"We do one thing, and we've been doing it a long time."*

### Body copy
First-person plural ("we"), conversational, specific examples.

### CTAs (calls to action)
Casual, low-pressure. We invite conversations, we don't demand action.
- *"Start a Conversation"*
- *"Get in Touch"*
- *"Curious what this could do for your community?"*

NOT:
- ❌ "Schedule Your Free Demo Today!"
- ❌ "Download Our Whitepaper Now!"
- ❌ "Don't Miss Out — Limited Time!"

### Form copy
Lower the bar. Make it easy. Honest about what happens next.
- *"Share as much or as little as you'd like."*
- *"We'll never share your information. Ever."*
- *"We answer most emails within the business day."*

### Direct mail and ads
Lead with feeling, not features. Amenities lose to a quiet morning on the porch.

## When clients ask "Make it more X"

### "Make it more professional"
We're already professional. They probably mean *more conservative*. Trim the dryness, slightly more formal sentence structure, but keep the warmth.

### "Make it warmer"
Add specificity to people. Mention residents, adult children, staff by what they're feeling, not what they're buying.

### "Make it punchier"
Shorter sentences. Stronger verbs. Cut the connective tissue.

### "Make it more AI-forward"
Increase specific AI capabilities mentioned. Do **not** add buzzwords. The way we make AI more visible is by being more concrete about what it actually does.

### "Make it more upscale"
Reduce humor slightly. Add restraint. Use more white space in the design. Don't suddenly add words like "luxury" or "exclusive" — show, don't tell.

## Voice anti-patterns to watch for

When AI is helping draft copy, watch out for these tells:

- **Em-dash overuse** — like this — happens too often — and gets exhausting
- **"It's not just X, it's Y"** construction
- **Triplet rhythms everywhere** ("Smart. Strategic. Successful.") — fine occasionally, exhausting in volume
- **Empty validators** — "It's important to note that...", "In today's fast-paced world..."
- **Reflexive bothsidesing** — "While X is true, Y is also true" when only one is relevant
- **Vague enthusiasm** — "Exciting news!", "We're thrilled to announce!"
- **Closing flourishes that say nothing** — "...and that's what makes all the difference."

If a draft has more than one or two of these, rewrite it.
